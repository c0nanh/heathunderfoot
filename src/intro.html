This is a walk round the edge of a piece of ground that was defended in Parliament seven times, bought twice by public subscription, quarried for sand, dammed for drinking water, painted by Constable and walked by Keats. It is nine and a half kilometres, and it starts and ends at a memorial to a tree that is not there.

The twenty stops are numbered in route order. Each one tells you where to stand and what to look at, then tells you the story. Where a well-loved story turns out to be a legend, it says so, with a stamp. Nothing in here is invented, which is more than can be said for some of the pubs.
